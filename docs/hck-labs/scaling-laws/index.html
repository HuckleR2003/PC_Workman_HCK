What you can do to a lab

  • Read its dossier, founded date and chapters as they happen, never ahead of time.
  • Buy its shares. Hold a majority and buy it out: you get its newest model and up to three quarters of its following.
  • Hire its people with an offer they can refuse.
  • Pay for stories about it, in four tiers. A traced campaign earns a legal letter, a phone call and possibly a lawsuit.
  • Sue it, and watch the odds fall as the demand rises.
  • Every action moves how the lab feels about you, whether or not it is ever traced back.

Starting small

It starts in a house, not a glass tower.

The first company view is deliberately modest: a desk, a bed, a living room, a garage and a cheap car. Later company growth is meant to become visible as well as numerical, with larger offices, staff and owned infrastructure replacing the starter setup.

The founder walks between the desk, the bed and the garage, staff sit at their desks during the hours you give them, offices are researched, rented and then bought, and the basement fills with cabinets. The city of Bayview around those buildings is finished and ships in the next build.
The loop

Research. Train. Release. Survive the bill.

Scaling Laws is built around decisions that consume both money and calendar. A better number is useful only if the company can afford the time it took to get there.

01 / RESEARCH

Open the option

Architectures, corpora, model types and infrastructure sit behind research. Cash helps. It does not erase prerequisite chains or calendar time.

02 / TRAIN

Commit compute

Choose the model, scale, data and compute budget. The creator shows projected capability, training time and the cash the run is expected to burn.

03 / RELEASE

Choose the day

A finished run can sit on the shelf. Waiting may dodge a rival launch or buy time for an upgrade, but the market keeps moving while you wait.

04 / BUSINESS

Make it pay

Set pricing, manage free access, build brand and fund the next cycle. A company that ships one model and coasts is designed to fall behind.

Under the interface

Real rules, coarse enough to play.

Scaling Laws uses published scaling-law behaviour and public hardware data as reference points, then turns them into a game economy. Estimates and future projections are meant to stay labelled as estimates.

~20×

Tokens per parameter

Compute-optimal training sits around twenty tokens per parameter in the model used by the game. Undertraining or overtraining makes the same compute budget buy a worse result.

10× → +10

Compute treadmill

Ten times the effective compute buys about ten capability points on the game's relative scale. The frontier therefore keeps moving even when the player's last model felt expensive.

2 ways

Hardware loses value

Owned hardware depreciates with time and again when meaningful successor generations arrive. Buying too early can leave capital trapped in a fleet the market has already passed.

Questions people ask

Is Scaling Laws free?

Yes. Every build so far is free for Windows on itch.io. A Steam release is planned for October to November 2026.

What is the current version?

Version 0.4.0, released 13 September 2026, an alpha. A city map of Bayview, rebuilt offices, a server room climate and tokenization research are finished for the next build.

Which AI labs are in the game?

Fourteen parody labs: OpenSI, Antropic, DeepThink, Infinity, Astral, DeepSearch, zAI, Swen, Grob, StableAI, IntroduceAI, Algho Alpha, Gohere, and E-Solutions, the invented lab of the player's cousin in Radom. Thirteen are seeded from the real 2022 to 2026 release timeline.

Can I buy or attack rival labs?

Yes. You can buy shares and take over a lab with a majority, hire its staff, pay for smear stories in four tiers, and sue it. Rivals can sue back. Every action changes the relationship.

Are alliances or mergers in the game?

Not yet. Alliances between labs and mergers are planned and not in any build.

Is the source code open source?

It is source available, not open source. The code can be read on GitHub, but from 19 September 2026 it may not be redistributed. Earlier versions stay under PolyForm Noncommercial 1.0.0.

Who makes Scaling Laws?

Marcin "HCK" Firmuga, a solo developer from Radom, Poland, publishing as HCK Labs. He also made PC Workman, a Windows monitor on the Microsoft Store.
