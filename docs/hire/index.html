How a build actually goes

Written out so you know where you can stop, before you pay rather than after. This is the shape of an eighteen day application; the smaller jobs are the same idea, shorter.

Days 0 to 1

We agree, in one sentence, on the single thing that makes the whole job worth doing. I write it back to you in my own words so you can tell me if I misunderstood, while misunderstanding still costs nothing. I also tell you anything I think will be harder than it looks.

Days 2 to 4 · you can stop here

You get a short recording of that one thing actually working. It will be ugly, plain and do exactly one job. If it is not what you meant, we change direction while almost nothing is built, or you cancel. I would rather lose three days than deliver the wrong thing on day eighteen.

Days 5 to 12

The rest gets built around it. I message you when I hit a decision I cannot make for you, and not about anything else. If something turns out bigger than we thought, you hear it the day I find out, with options.

Days 13 to 15 · you can stop here too

You get a build to install and break. Use it the way you really intend to, not the way a demo goes. Everything you find in this window is fixed inside the price.

Days 16 to 18

Fixes, packaging, and documentation written so that you, or whoever works on this after me, can pick it up without needing to ask me anything.

What I will not do

  • Tell you it takes ten days when it takes eighteen. If what you need is bigger than what you are paying for, I will say so before you order and describe the smaller honest version instead.
  • Take a job I am not right for. I have turned work down and I will again. A refund on day one is cheaper for both of us than a bad delivery on day twenty.
  • Disappear. If my own week goes wrong you hear it from me with a new date, not from a delivery that quietly does not arrive.
