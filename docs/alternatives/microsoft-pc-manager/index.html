PC Workman vs Microsoft PC Manager: a Boost button, or the numbers behind it

PC Manager is Microsoft's free helper: a Boost button, storage tools and fixes for the taskbar and pop-ups. PC Workman goes further where PC Manager stops: it measures before and after, says what takes your disk, watches temperatures and keeps months of history.

Where PC Manager is better

  • From Microsoft, in the Microsoft Store, nothing to trust beyond Windows itself.
  • Quick fixes PC Workman does not do: taskbar repair, pop-up management in apps, default browser protection.
  • Duplicate file finder in its storage tools.

Where PC Workman is better

  • Measured, not promised. In PCMag's test PC Manager's Boost lowered memory use by about 5% and raised a PCMark 10 score by 9 points (summary). PC Workman writes a receipt with the numbers before and after every action.
  • What takes the disk, explained: each big folder with what it is and what to do, and free space over time.
  • Monitoring: temperatures against a learned normal, DeepMonitor, history.
  • Startup and services with reasons, and hck_GPT to ask in plain words.

Who should use which

PC Manager for quick Windows fixes from Microsoft. PC Workman when you want to know what is happening: numbers before and after, the disk explained, temperatures and history.

Using both

They do not conflict. Use PC Manager's taskbar and pop-up fixes, and PC Workman for cleanup with a receipt, monitoring and history. See Cleanup and TURBO.

Questions

Does PC Manager's Boost really speed up the PC?

A little and briefly. In PCMag's test memory use fell about 5% and a benchmark moved 9 points. Freeing memory helps only when the PC is short of it.

Is there an alternative to PC Manager with more detail?

PC Workman: cleanup with a file list, a disk analysis, startup and services with reasons, temperatures and history, all with numbers before and after.

Can I use PC Manager and PC Workman together?

Yes.
