PC Workman vs HWMonitor: the readings, or what they mean

HWMonitor opens in a second and shows a clean tree of sensors with minimum and maximum. PC Workman keeps that idea in DeepMonitor and adds what HWMonitor does not have: a normal learned for this PC, months of history and answers in plain words.

Where HWMonitor is better

  • Tiny and instant. A small program that shows everything at once, no learning time.
  • Wide sensor support from CPUID, the makers of CPU-Z.
  • CSV logging with F5 in the free version; HWMonitor PRO adds a graph generator and remote monitoring (CPUID).

Where PC Workman is better

  • A normal for this PC, learned per workload, so a reading is judged against the same kind of load.
  • History without set-up: 7 days by the minute, 90 days by the hour, daily for good.
  • One CPU temperature chosen for you, and false sensors (127 °C on AUXTIN) marked.
  • Explanations from hck_GPT and links to what fixes the problem.

Who should use which

HWMonitor for a quick look at the numbers right now. PC Workman to know whether those numbers are normal for your PC and how they changed over weeks.

Using both

Keep HWMonitor for the quick glance and let PC Workman run in the tray to build the history and the norm. See DeepMonitor.

Questions

Does HWMonitor keep history?

The free version logs to CSV when you start it with F5; HWMonitor PRO adds graphs. PC Workman keeps history by itself for months.

What is a good free alternative to HWMonitor?

PC Workman, if you want history and a learned normal on top of the readings; HWiNFO, if you want even more sensors.

Can I use HWMonitor and PC Workman together?

Yes, they do not conflict.

How to open it

Try PC Workman next to HWMonitor: after a few days of normal use, the norm and the history are there.
