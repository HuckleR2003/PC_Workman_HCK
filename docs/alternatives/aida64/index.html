PC Workman vs AIDA64 Extreme: every detail, or what to do first

AIDA64 Extreme lists every detail of every device, benchmarks the PC, stress-tests it and drives custom sensor panels, for about 60 USD. PC Workman is free and does the everyday part: a Health Report you read in a minute, a normal learned for your PC, a map of its parts and months of history.

Where AIDA64 is better

  • Detail: hardware and software detection down to every device and setting, and 250+ sensors.
  • Benchmarks of the CPU, memory and caches, comparable with other PCs.
  • System Stability Test that loads CPU, memory, drives and the GPU.
  • SensorPanel: custom gauges and graphs, often on a small second screen (AIDA64).
  • Long reports a repair shop or an IT department may need.

Where PC Workman is better

  • Free and open source, against about 60 USD for AIDA64 Extreme after its trial.
  • A report you act on: a score out of 100 from six parts and what to do first, saved as one HTML page.
  • A learned normal per workload, so readings are judged against your PC, not a table.
  • History built in, and a PC Map that draws your parts and colours them by temperature.
  • hck_GPT to ask about any of it in plain words.

Who should use which

AIDA64 for benchmarks, full inventories and a custom SensorPanel. PC Workman to know whether the PC is healthy, what to fix first and how it behaves over time, for free.

Using both

Use AIDA64 when you need a benchmark or a full inventory, and let PC Workman keep the history and the norm the rest of the time. See Health Report.

Questions

Is there a free alternative to AIDA64?

For everyday health checks, yes: PC Workman gives a health score, what to do first, a learned normal and history, free and open source. For benchmarks and full inventories, AIDA64 stays the tool.

How much does AIDA64 Extreme cost?

About 60 USD for a licence after the trial, depending on the offer.

Does PC Workman have a stability test like AIDA64?

PC Workman's Stability Tests check CPU, memory and drives against the PC's own normal; AIDA64's test covers more components and is aimed at enthusiasts.
