Speed up Windows in six measured steps, without the myths

Most "speed up your PC" lists promise and never measure. The Optimization Guide starts with a baseline from your PC, walks through startup, services, memory and drivers, and ends by showing the same numbers again, so you see what each step actually did.

The six steps

  1. Baseline. RAM, CPU and the number of processes right now. These numbers come back at the end.
  2. Startup. What starts with Windows, with a keep, delay or disable answer for each entry; you choose, the guide applies.
  3. Services. Running services most PCs do not need (telemetry, Xbox services, fax, remote registry), named one by one.
  4. Memory. What holds the RAM now, and whether freeing working memory helps on this PC or only looks good.
  5. Result. The baseline again, side by side, and a list of what was switched off in this run.
  6. Drivers. The components and the age of their drivers, with where to update them safely.

Each step can be skipped, and you can ask a question in the middle ("what is SysMain?") without losing your place.

Measured, with a receipt

Every action PC Workman takes leaves an Optimization Receipt: what was done, the numbers before and after, and how to undo it. A receipt that says "freed 0.4 GB, no speed difference to expect" is a valid receipt. The point is to know, not to feel faster.

Questions

How do I speed up my PC safely?

Start with what starts with Windows and what runs in the background, measure before and after, and change one thing at a time. The Optimization Guide does exactly that in six steps.

Will optimization make games faster?

Sometimes a little: fewer programs in the background mean fewer stutters. If the graphics card is at 100% in a game, only lower settings or a better card help, and the guide says so.

Can I undo what the guide changed?

Yes. Startup entries are switched off the way Task Manager does it, services return with one click, and every action has a receipt.
