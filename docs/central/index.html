Who it is for

  • School computer labs. Twenty to forty PCs, one IT teacher, no time to walk from desk to desk with a thermometer.
  • Small offices. Ten to fifty PCs and nobody whose job is to notice that one of them is about to run out of disk.
  • Internet cafés and gaming rooms. Machines under load all day, where a clogged cooler shows up as a customer complaint.
  • Computer repair shops. A quick, honest health picture of every machine that comes in.

What the admin sees

A tile for every PC with its state colour, its name and three numbers: CPU temperature, memory, and the system drive. Click a tile and the full PC Workman of that machine opens, read only: My PC, its history, its processes.

Warnings in plain words, written by hck_GPT for the admin, for example:

PC-14: drive C: has 4% free, Windows updates will not fit.
PC-07: CPU 15 °C hotter than its own normal under the same load. Check for dust.
PC-22: three restarts without a shutdown in a week.

Each PC is judged against its own learned normal, the same way DeepMonitor does it on a single PC, so a lab of different machines does not drown in false alarms from one fixed threshold.

  • Comparisons: lab averages, the five worst PCs, the one that runs hotter than its identical twins, the age of drivers across the lab.
  • History of the whole lab for more than six months, with the busy hours (lessons, shifts) visible.
  • A report for the head or the owner: the state of the hardware, what to replace first, which PCs go to service.

What it never collects

Classroom tools mostly watch people: screens, websites, what a pupil types. That is where the trouble starts. In Poland the data protection authority fined a school 52,511 PLN for cameras in classrooms without a legal basis and ordered them off within 14 days (Prawo.pl). Central watches hardware, not people.

  • School mode: programs are shown as groups (browsers, games, office) instead of a full list, so the data cannot tell what one pupil was doing.
  • A visible agent: every PC says it is part of a lab managed by PC Workman Central. There is no hidden mode.
  • A pack for the head and the data protection officer: the list of collected data, an information notice template for pupils, parents and staff, a template of the data protection impact assessment (GDPR art. 35), and a default retention period that can be shortened.

How it works

  1. The agent is PC Workman running without a window on every PC. It collects locally all the time, learns that machine's normal and keeps its history, even when Central is off.
  2. Central runs on the admin's PC and only reads from the agents.
  3. Pairing with a code, like Bluetooth: a code on the agent, typed into Central. The connection is encrypted with a key from the pairing, so nobody else on the network can read another PC.
  4. Local network only in version 1. No cloud, no accounts. The data does not leave the building.
  5. Version 1 is view only. No remote killing of processes, restarts or installs: a remote action on forty PCs is the biggest risk a lab can take. Remote actions may come later, behind permissions and with a log.
  6. Quiet install for admins: an installer with parameters (silent install, pairing code), so forty PCs are not a day of clicking.

Fair to say: Veyon and NetSupport School run a lesson: they show and lock screens, and Central never will. NinjaOne and other remote management tools patch and fix machines remotely. Central does one thing they leave out: it tells you, before a lesson breaks, which PC is about to fail and why. Many schools will run Central next to a classroom tool, not instead of it.

Questions

Does it watch what pupils or employees do?

No. Hardware state only: temperatures, load, drives, drivers, and programs as names with average use. Never screens, keystrokes, websites, file contents or names, or Windows user names. In school mode programs are shown as groups.

Is it compatible with GDPR in a school?

It is built for it: hardware data only, on the school's own network, a visible agent, and a pack for the head and the data protection officer. The school stays the controller and decides on the legal basis.

Does it need the internet or a cloud account?

No. Local network only, pairing with a code, encrypted connection. The data does not leave the building.

Can it restart or fix PCs remotely?

Not in version 1, on purpose. It shows and explains. Remote actions may come later, behind permissions and with a log.

How much will it cost?

Not set yet. The plan: free for a few PCs, a yearly licence per lab above that, an education licence for public schools, well below business remote management tools. Pilot places are free.

When will it be available?

It is in preparation. The first step is a pilot in one school lab or one small office.
